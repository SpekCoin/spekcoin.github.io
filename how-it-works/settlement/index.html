How it works

Settlement

Spek has three settlement layers and states each for what it is. There is no finality gadget, and nothing on this page pretends there is.

The 4-block fork window

Each block adds its score to its chain's cumulative score, and the heavier chain wins. When a competing branch forks within 4 blocks of the tip, both branches are compared by cumulative score once each holds at least two blocks above the fork point, and the loser is dropped. Waiting for both sides avoids switching back and forth on every block. The reorg is checked against a copy of the ledger before any live state changes, and rollback on this path is bounded at 5 blocks.

Forks deeper than the window, such as the two sides of a healed network partition, go to a full download that finds the true branch point, compares cumulative score, and reorgs at any depth above the node's restoration pin.

Confirmations by attacker share

Draws are fixed per height, so an attacker can rewrite history only by out-scoring the honest chain over the same heights. The tables below give the probability that a transaction is ever reversible, and the confirmations needed for under one reversible slot per century, against an attacker holding share q of the draws who may choose its fork point in hindsight. Integrators choose their depth from the share they want to tolerate: for example 136 confirmations, about 68 minutes, against an attacker holding a third.

The same numbers, in plain words

A confirmation is one more block built on top of yours, so it takes 30 seconds. q is the share of all the draws an attacker controls: 0.33 means one draw in three. The attacker is assumed to do the most damaging thing possible, looking back over the whole chain to pick the best moment to start a hidden branch.

How long to wait. Here "reversal" means the attacker's hidden branch overtakes the honest chain at some moment, even briefly, which is a harsher test than keeping control. The wait in the table is chosen so that, counting every one of the 1,051,200 slots in a year, the expected number of transactions ever reversed is less than one in a hundred years. The odds for any single transaction are far smaller than that, because the hundred years and a million slots a year are spread across all of them.

How the risk falls as you wait. The odds of a reversal drop very fast with each confirmation. For an attacker holding a third of the draws: after 6 confirmations (3 minutes) about 1 in 4 reversals succeed; after 30 (15 minutes) about 1 in 140; after 120 (an hour) about 1 in 16 million. For an attacker holding 10%, 6 confirmations already leave about 1 chance in 2,800, and after 30 it is effectively never. Once an attacker holds close to half, no wait is safe for long, which is why the table stops at 45%.

Restoration pins

A running node never reorganises below its restoration pin: the newest checkpoint boundary buried at least one full interval below its tip. Checkpoint boundaries sit every 4,000 blocks (about 33 hours), so the pin trails the tip by 33–67 hours.

The checkpoint barrier

A node with no history of its own, such as a new node or one that was offline for days, trusts the checkpoints compiled into its release and pin files signed by a threshold of checkpoint signers. A fork whose branch point lies behind the node's restoration pin or a hard checkpoint is never resolved automatically, and the peer offering it is banned.

Why checkpoints are permanent

Unbonded stake cannot bound signing after the fact: a key that held stake at a fork point can sign a branch from that point at any later time, even after its owner sold the coins. Hard checkpoints are therefore permanent weak subjectivity, not a launch measure, and are never retired. A node checks a pin file itself: it must name the network and genesis, carry signatures from a threshold of the checkpoint signers compiled into the binary, and may add to the compiled-in table but never contradict it.

No finality gadget

There is no finality gadget and no rollback limit shorter than the pin; the reasons are on Design choices. Coinbase outputs mature after 3,000 blocks, about 25 hours.
