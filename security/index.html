SQIsign standardisation status

SQIsign is not a NIST standard. NIST advanced nine candidates to Round 3 of its additional digital-signature process in May 2026; one of them, HAWK, has since been withdrawn by its submission team, leaving eight, and SQIsign is among them. That round is expected to last about two years, a draft standard could not appear before about 2028, and SQIsign may be dropped. "Level I" and "Level V" refer to the security categories the parameters target, not to any NIST approval. This section is updated at each NIST milestone.

If later cryptanalysis weakens SQIsign, or NIST drops it, Spek's only remedy is a migration to another signature scheme through a hard fork, and every operator chooses for themselves whether to adopt it. The reasons for choosing SQIsign, and what a Level I break would and would not allow, are on the Cryptography page.

Security claims

What Spek claims, and under what assumptions. This is section 2.1 of the white paper, verbatim.

What Spek claims, and under what assumptions, in one place. Each claim points to the section that argues it.

Assumptions. SQIsign Level I and BLAKE3 are secure (§8). Honest nodes take their time from a Roughtime pool whose operators are mostly honest (§3.3); colluding operators can walk a node's clock by at most 5 s per sync (§17). Headers reach honest nodes within the 6-second headers-only phase and bodies within the 24-second block phase (§3.2). The attacker holds a share q of the draws in play, a fraction of all funded stake addresses after the launch phase and of recorded tickets during it (§7).

Claims.

  1. Unpredictable winners. Nobody but a key's holder can compute its score before its header is published (§4.1, §4.3).
  2. No retries. For each stake address (key, horizon root and horizon start) and height exactly one draw secret verifies, so a miner cannot improve its score by trying again (§4.1, §4.7). The argument reduces a second verifying draw secret to a BLAKE3 collision (§4.8).
  3. Bounded beacon bias. A miner's only informed influence on a beacon is withholding blocks at the end of the beacon's source epoch, worth 1.9–3.0% of its own wins in an epoch it controls and 0.3–0.6% averaged over all epochs, with no compounding (§4.2). Withholding earlier in that epoch changes the beacon blindly.
  4. Blocks in proportion to draws. A party with share q of the draws wins share q of the slots (§1, §7.3).
  5. Confirmations. Against q = 1/3, 223 confirmations (1.9 hours) keep reversals under one slot per century; Appendix B gives the depth for q up to 0.40. The model assumes the attacker's branch shares the honest chain's beacons, which holds for its first 1,441 blocks. Forcing null blocks on the honest chain gains an attacker nothing below about q = 0.44 (§6.1).
  6. Rewrite bounds. A running node never reorganises below its restoration pin (33–67 h deep). A private branch inside the pin gains on the honest chain only above an attacker share of 0.44–0.46, and a fresh node with no checkpoint past the fork is safe only below about 0.38 (§17).
  7. Liveness. Every slot ends with a block, a null block if no valid candidate arrives (§5). An attacker can force a null only by holding all five of a slot's best headers and withholding their bodies, about q⁵ of slots (§17), and below about q = 0.44 doing so does not help a private branch (§6.1).

Working assumption. Spek is designed to be safe for q < 0.38, and its confirmation guidance is sized for q ≤ 1/3 (223 confirmations). Above 0.38 the protocol makes no safety claim for nodes without recent checkpoints.

Not claimed. Fast finality; safety above half the draws; security once rewards stop and only fees pay miners (§17); any guarantee for a node that trusts no checkpoint (§12.3).

Threat model

Each threat, the mechanism that addresses it, and what remains. This is section 15 of the white paper, verbatim; the open problems are in section 17, also on the Limitations page.

Sybil (post-launch) One output ≥ 80% of reward per draw, aged 24–36 h Attack share = capital share; concentration risk is economic, not protocol
Sybil (launch phase) RandomX ticket per stake address per 3-day round, recorded on chain before it counts; RandomX with Spek's own parameters, so hardware built for other RandomX chains does not apply Launch seats: the search opens at a Bitcoin block, so every searcher starts together and no record exists before it; the organiser could leave a listed entry out of the sealed list, which the public listings and the witnessed seal digest expose. No party has an informed abort: the launch beacons depend on a Bitcoin block mined after the seal
Private-branch rewrite Fixed draws; pins bound running nodes; checkpoints bound fresh nodes A private branch chooses its own beacons: break-even attacker share 0.44–0.46 within the pin; about 0.38 for a fresh node without checkpoints

Clock steering Authenticated Roughtime quorum, operator diversity, raw-clock projection, 5 s step bound, mining hold A quorate colluding cohort can walk the clock 5 s per sync; pool composition is the security parameter (§17). Roughtime servers sign with Ed25519, which is not post-quantum: a quantum attacker could forge time. This is known, and there are a few years to address it
Equivocation One entry per key per attempt; different content → slot cancelled, proof gossiped None beyond the forfeited slot

Winning power is unaffected by flood rate
Stake-address dust One counted output per draw, value-ordered index Each extra step costs a full stake unit
Validation-cost amplification Per-input signature budget charged before verify; Level V witness bytes weighted by ρ; cache of valid signatures; full validation for top-ranked candidates only Adversarial leaf ≈ 1.27× honest signature density
Deep reorg / long range Depth-4 comparison, bounded rollback, untruncated chainwork, restoration pins, checkpoint barrier See the next row for partitions
Long network partition Both sides keep mining; a partition shorter than the pin lag heals by a deep reorganisation to the heavier side (§6.2) A partition longer than the pin lag (33–67 h) splits the network permanently: each side pins its own branch and refuses the other as a checkpoint divergence, and only operators can rejoin them (§17)
Fake-chain mining halt Mining never paused by download None
Eclipse during sync 8-peer minimum (1 with a built-in bootstrap node), diversity caps, no relaxing flag Small deployments depend on bootstrap reachability for deep-fork resolution
Witness malleability Identities exclude witness bytes: transaction ids and block hashes do not cover signatures, the merkle root commits to the exact witnesses; canonical encoding, minimal numbers, clean stack, minimal conditionals, one signature rule SQIsign signatures themselves stay malleable: a mempool copy may differ from the one mined, which costs a re-verification, never an identity. An attacker who relays altered copies can make compact block reconstruction miss, so the block falls back to a full download, which the 24 s body window is sized for
